# Product Philosophy

This document states the beliefs behind Make Dating Free Again. It explains what kind of relationship the product should
have with its Users; enforceable product rules belong in [principles.md](./principles.md).

## Attention Is a Cost, Not the Product's Value

The product creates value when it helps people make progress toward a Meaningful Outcome. Time spent, repeated visits,
Recommendations viewed, Conversations, and Match count may describe usage, but they do not define success.

A dating product should be comfortable becoming unnecessary once it has helped a User achieve their goal.

## People Have Dignity and Agency

People are not inventory, rankings, or optimization targets. Compatibility can be assessed only in the context of two
people's intentions, preferences, and Boundaries; it is never a measure of either person's worth.

Dating includes uncertainty and rejection. The product's role is to preserve each person's ability to decide, decline,
leave a Live Conversation, end contact, and control access to their information without turning those choices into
public judgments.

## The Product Facilitates Two Different Decisions

The product helps each User make two distinct decisions:

1. **Preview Interest:** am I willing to try a short Live Conversation with this Candidate now?
2. **Continue Decision:** after that Conversation, do I want to continue the connection?

The first decision must never be presented as a Match or as consent to persistent communication. The second remains
private unless both Users choose to continue. Keeping these decisions separate lets the product facilitate discovery
without manufacturing interest or replacing consent.

## The Product Is a Facilitator

The product creates conditions for mutual discovery and synchronous communication. It does not choose a partner,
manufacture human interest, promise a relationship, or substitute automation for consent.

Its role is to reduce avoidable friction between a deliberate Recommendation Decision, a mutually accepted Live
Conversation, and the next voluntary step. It must remain acceptable for either User to stop at any point.

## Models Are Fallible

A Recommendation or Compatibility Assessment is a hypothesis, not a verdict. Product behavior should remain honest
about uncertainty and about what available data cannot establish.

Explicit Dating Intent, Boundaries, Live Availability, and User decisions deserve greater trust than speculative
conclusions drawn from engagement behavior.

## Evidence Can Change the Product

The mission does not make the synchronous-conversation hypothesis true. Research and experiments should be able to
disconfirm it, and the product should change if early live contact does not improve informed outcomes or creates
unacceptable anxiety, exclusion, safety risk, or operational cost.

The current evidence base and its limits are documented in [research.md](./research.md).

## Economic Incentives Should Align With User Outcomes

Financial sustainability is necessary, but a dating product should not benefit from preventing Users from reaching
their goals. A viable business model should create value when Users make healthy progress, not when they remain trapped
in unproductive browsing, waiting, or communication loops.
